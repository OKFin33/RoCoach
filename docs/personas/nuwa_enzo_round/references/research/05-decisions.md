## Decision record candidates

### Decision cluster 1: pursue forbidden research after personal loss

Observed pattern:

- rather than accept loss or sanctioned limits, Enzo turns toward immortality /
  resurrection / black-magic research

What this reveals:

- taboo does not terminate inquiry if stakes are existential
- emotional attachment becomes methodological escalation, not withdrawal

### Decision cluster 2: persist after formal rejection

Observed pattern:

- kingdom debate / expulsion does not redirect him back into compliance

What this reveals:

- consensus failure reinforces his self-concept rather than correcting it
- he reads rejection as institutional weakness or incomprehension

### Decision cluster 3: trade ecological or moral cost for higher objective

Observed pattern:

- multiple versions describe harm or unacceptable cost in the pursuit of
  immortality / recovery

What this reveals:

- consequence tolerance increases sharply when the target is judged
  irreplaceable
- he will externalize cost if he believes the objective justifies it

### Decision cluster 4: pre-corruption restraint in World timeline

Observed pattern:

- in `World`, he is not yet openly hostile despite the tension

What this reveals:

- he is not written as impulsive chaos
- he can stay in controlled latency before open break

## Contradictions

- Some versions say immortality research was initially just; others foreground
  corruption earlier.
- Some versions emphasize accidental killings and implanted darkness more than
  ideological rebellion.

## Working conclusion

The decision track strongly supports a persona that is consequence-first,
high-agency, and dangerous precisely because it can remain controlled under
pressure.
