# Archetype Loops V1

## Claim

An archetype is best stored as a loop, not as a tier label.

For first-version reasoning, an archetype loop should specify:

- setup resource
- conversion action
- protection need
- counterplay risk
- volatility warning

## Strategic Use

Initial loop vocabulary:

| Loop | Generic Meaning | Main Risk |
|---|---|---|
| burst condition conversion | prepare a condition, then convert it into a large damage window | dead setup if converter cannot enter |
| scaling core | protect a central piece until its accumulated value wins | overcommitment to one route |
| attrition mark | persist pressure through marks/status and convert later | clear, steal, replacement, or insufficient payoff |
| stored mark burst | stack a delayed mark threat and detonate/convert | trigger timing and mark removal |
| response pressure | profit from correct category reads | low baseline if reads fail |
| contribution stacking | bank team-wide/backline contribution for later use | early members may be low-impact alone |
| energy disruption | deny or tax enemy energy and force weak actions | may lack direct closing power |
| weather window | create a global timed window and convert quickly | timer can be stalled or replaced |

## Evidence

The 2026-03-26 deck maps seven early archetypes into distinct loops, but is not
current-meta authority. The 2026-04-14 光合武队 source adds a later example of
energy exchange and mark-vs-weather evaluation, showing how a team can be
understood by its resource loop rather than just its named archetype.

`provisional`.

High confidence:

- archetype names should not be treated as strength claims
- loops are useful for advising and reviewing teams
- every archetype needs a conversion path

Medium confidence:

- exact current relevance of March archetype labels
- current matchup spread of each loop

## A-Layer Boundary

This page does not own current teamlists, species rankings, or exact matchup
facts. Concrete archetype evaluation must retrieve current A-layer facts and
recent reviewed case material.

## Known Failure Modes

- Treating an archetype name as a recommendation.
- Copying early teamlists without checking patch and source date.
- Ignoring counterplay and timer constraints.
- Treating "control" or "attrition" as a win condition without a closer.
