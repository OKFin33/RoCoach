# Core Team Construction

## Claim

A PvP team should be read as a conversion system, not a list of individually
strong spirits.

The advisor should identify:

- the primary win condition
- the setup route
- the protection route
- the conversion route
- the fallback route when the main route is denied

## Strategic Use

For each proposed team, ask:

- What does this team spend turns and energy to create?
- Which member turns that resource into KOs, attrition, or a decisive board
  state?
- Which member protects the engine from clear, steal, forced switch, or bad
  matchup pressure?
- Which member covers the core's defensive weaknesses?
- What does the team do if the opponent refuses the intended interaction?

This framing prevents two common mistakes:

- recommending six strong spirits with no shared plan
- overbuilding around one carry without entry, protection, and fallback logic

## Evidence

The 2026-03-26 archetype deck repeatedly describes teams by loop: burst
condition conversion, core-first construction, poison/mark attrition, starfall
stored threat, response-based martial pressure, bug-team contribution stacking,
and ghost energy pressure.

The champion-team-logic source frames team positions as output, support, and
interception/counter roles. It emphasizes that team construction starts from a
core and then patches weaknesses and matchup needs.

The defensive-switching/prediction source provides early material for treating
switching and coverage as team structure rather than isolated move choice.

## A-Layer Boundary

This page does not own exact species, move, ability, or matchup facts. It should
call A-layer data when judging whether a specific member can actually perform a
role.

## Known Failure Modes

- Treating early-release archetype ratings as current strength.
- Ignoring energy cost and setup turn count.
- Ignoring mark/weather/response counterplay.
- Assuming a core can enter safely without route support.
- Recommending a team without explaining its conversion path.
